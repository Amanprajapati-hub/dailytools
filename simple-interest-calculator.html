What Is Simple Interest?

Simple interest is a method of calculating interest based on the original amount of money, the interest rate and the length of time. Unlike compound interest, simple interest is calculated only on the original principal amount.

The calculation remains straightforward because the interest does not earn additional interest during the selected period. The same principal amount is used throughout the calculation.

This Simple Interest Calculator helps you quickly find both the interest amount and the total amount after adding the interest to the original principal.

Simple Interest Formula

The standard formula for calculating simple interest is:

Simple Interest = (P × R × T) ÷ 100

P represents the principal amount, R represents the annual interest rate and T represents the time period in years.

After calculating the interest, the total amount can be found by adding the simple interest to the original principal.

Total Amount = Principal + Simple Interest

Understanding the Calculation Values

The original amount of money used as the base for the interest calculation.

Interest Rate

The percentage used to calculate the interest for one year.

Time Period

The length of time used in the calculation, entered in years.

Total Amount

The final value after adding the calculated simple interest to the principal amount.

Simple Interest Calculation Example

Suppose the principal amount is 10,000, the annual interest rate is 5% and the time period is 2 years.

Principal: 10,000

Interest Rate: 5% per year

Time: 2 years

Simple Interest = (10,000 × 5 × 2) ÷ 100

Simple Interest = 1,000

The total amount is the principal plus the interest: 10,000 + 1,000 = 11,000.

Simple Interest and Compound Interest

Simple interest and compound interest use different methods for calculating interest. With simple interest, the calculation is based on the original principal amount for the entire period.

Compound interest can include interest calculated on previously accumulated interest. As a result, compound interest may produce a different total amount over longer periods.

It is important to check which interest method applies before estimating a final amount.
