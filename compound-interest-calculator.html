What Is Compound Interest?

Compound interest is calculated on the original principal amount as well as the interest accumulated from previous periods. This means that interest can earn additional interest over time.

The final amount depends on the principal, annual interest rate, investment duration and compounding frequency. More frequent compounding can produce a different result compared with annual compounding, assuming the same rate and duration.

The DailyTools Compound Interest Calculator helps calculate the accumulated interest and final amount using these values.

Compound Interest Formula

The standard compound interest formula is:

A = P × (1 + r/n)nt

Compound Interest = A − P

A is the final amount, P is the principal, r is the annual interest rate in decimal form, n is the number of compounding periods per year and t is the time in years.

The calculator converts the percentage interest rate into decimal form before applying the formula.

Understanding Compounding Frequency

Compounding frequency describes how often accumulated interest is added to the principal balance.

Annually

Interest is added once at the end of each year.

Half-Yearly

Interest is calculated and added twice each year.

Quarterly

Interest is compounded four times per year, once every three months.

Monthly

Interest is added twelve times per year.

Daily

Interest is compounded using 365 periods per year.

Compound Interest Calculation Example

Suppose an initial amount of 10,000 earns an annual interest rate of 8% for 5 years, compounded annually.

Principal: 10,000

Annual Interest Rate: 8%

Time Period: 5 years

Compounding Frequency: Annually

A = 10,000 × (1 + 0.08/1)1 × 5

Final Amount = 14,693.28

Compound Interest = 4,693.28

The accumulated interest is added to the balance during each compounding period, so the amount used to calculate later interest can increase.

Simple Interest vs. Compound Interest

Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal. Compound interest is calculated on the principal plus accumulated interest from earlier periods.

Simple Interest

The interest calculation is based on the original principal throughout the entire selected period.

Compound Interest

The calculation includes previously accumulated interest, allowing the balance to grow over time.

Factors That Affect Compound Interest

  • Principal: The initial amount used in the calculation.
  • Interest Rate: The annual percentage applied to the balance.
  • Time: The number of years the amount earns interest.
  • Compounding Frequency: How often accumulated interest is added to the balance.

Changing any of these values can change the final amount and accumulated interest.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Is the Compound Interest Calculator free?

2. What is the compound interest formula?

The formula is A = P × (1 + r/n) raised to the power of nt. Compound interest is the final amount minus the original principal.

3. What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is calculated on the original principal, while compound interest includes previously accumulated interest.

4. What does compounding frequency mean?

It describes how many times interest is added to the balance during one year.

5. Does monthly compounding differ from annual compounding?

Yes. With the same nominal annual rate and duration, monthly compounding generally produces a slightly higher final amount than annual compounding.
